You typed something like "affordable photographer Toronto" into Google because you have a real event coming up, a real budget, and no interest in paying wedding money for a two hour birthday party.

Earlier this year I added an hourly rate to my celebration packages: **$100 per hour, with 40 edited photos for every hour I shoot.** Book only the hours you need, travel within the GTA included. This post covers what the rate includes, what it does not, and how to decide if it fits your event.

For context, published Toronto pricing guides put typical event coverage between $200 and $500 per hour, and two or three hour minimums are common. I am not claiming those photographers are overpriced. Many of them are shooting six hour corporate galas with two shooters. I am saying a two hour birthday party is a different job, and it deserves a price built for it.

## Why I added a $100 per hour rate

My standard [Celebrations package](/services/celebrations/) starts at $551.25. It covers the first two hours, each additional hour is $151.25, and I edit every good frame from the event, unlimited. For a big milestone with a full program, that is still the package I recommend.

But I kept getting the same kind of inquiry. A two hour birthday dinner. A one hour cake smash at home. A small shower where the host mostly wanted the decor, the cake table, and twenty good photos of people laughing. Quoting a $551.25 package for that felt like selling a banquet to someone who asked for lunch.

So the hourly rate exists for exactly those events.

## What $100 per hour includes

- One photographer, me, for the hours you book. I am the same person who shoots my weddings, with the same cameras.
- **40 edited photos per hour of coverage.** Not 40 files dumped from a memory card. Chosen frames, edited one by one, the same way I finish wedding galleries.
- A private online gallery you can share with family and guests.
- Delivery within two weeks.
- Travel within the Greater Toronto Area at no extra charge. Outside the GTA I quote it upfront before you book.

Two hours at a birthday party comes to $200 and roughly 80 finished photos. That is the entire math. There is no fine print underneath it.

## What gets cut at this price, because something always does

When a photographer is cheap, something was cut. That is true everywhere, so the useful question is what got cut.

Here is my list, in full:

- **The photo count is capped.** 40 edited photos per hour instead of every good frame. At a lively party I shoot far more than that, so I pick the best of each moment rather than editing them all.
- **There is no film.** The hourly rate is photography only. Video is a different production.
- **There is no second shooter.** One angle, one photographer. For most parties under about 60 guests, that is genuinely all you need.

What did not get cut: the gear, the editing, the gallery, or how much I care about the people in the photos.

## What an afternoon of event coverage actually looks like

This summer I photographed [Zoha and Moe's twentieth anniversary](/portfolio/zoha-moe/) at a private condo party room in Maple, glass on two sides, sculptural branch chandeliers, a terrace wrapping the building. I arrived while the room was still quiet, tables going up, the bartender lining bottles along the dark wood bar. Then the doors opened and it stayed loud for the rest of the day. Family had flown in from around the world for one afternoon.

The frames that mattered were not posed. The welcome sign framed by sunflowers. A carved pineapple boat on the grazing table. Tea poured from a glass pot into little glasses beside the candles. A guest in a brown tweed suit holding one of those glasses, mid conversation. Grandparents and grandkids on the terrace couches in the late light.

That is what you are actually buying at any price point: someone who notices those moments while you are busy living them. The hourly rate just makes the entry price honest for smaller versions of that day.

## When the hourly rate is the wrong choice

I would rather talk you out of the wrong booking than take it. Skip the hourly rate if:

- **You want every moment edited.** The [Celebrations package](/services/celebrations/) is $551.25 for the first two hours and $151.25 for each hour after, and it costs more than the hourly rate at every length. You are not paying for the hours. You are paying to lift the 40 photo cap so I edit every good frame of the night. A four hour party is $400 and about 160 photos on the hourly rate, or $853.75 and unlimited on Celebrations. Pick based on how much of the night you want back.
- **It is a wedding.** Weddings are not hourly work, they are planned productions with scouting, timelines, and backups. I wrote a full honest breakdown of [what wedding photography costs in Toronto](/blog/intimate-toronto-wedding-photography-cost/) if that is the budget you are working on.
- **You need video too.** That is a different conversation, and I am happy to have it, but not at $100 per hour.
