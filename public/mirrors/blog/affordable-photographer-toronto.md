## What gets cut at this price, because something always does

When a photographer is cheap, something was cut. That is true everywhere, so the useful question is what got cut.

Here is my list, in full:

- **The photo count is capped.** 40 edited photos per hour instead of every good frame. At a lively party I shoot far more than that, so I pick the best of each moment rather than editing them all.
- **There is no film.** The hourly rate is photography only. Video is a different production.
- **There is no second shooter.** One angle, one photographer. For most parties under about 60 guests, that is genuinely all you need.

What did not get cut: the gear, the editing, the gallery, or how much I care about the people in the photos.

A guest in a brown tweed suit holding one of those glasses, mid conversation. Grandparents and grandkids on the terrace couches in the late light.

That is what you are actually buying at any price point: someone who notices those moments while you are busy living them. The hourly rate just makes the entry price honest for smaller versions of that day.

## When the hourly rate is the wrong choice

I would rather talk you out of the wrong booking than take it. Skip the hourly rate if:

- **You want every moment edited.** The [Celebrations package](/services/celebrations/) is $551.25 for the first two hours and $151.25 for each hour after, and it costs more than the hourly rate at every length. You are not paying for the hours. You are paying to lift the 40 photo cap so I edit every good frame of the night. A four hour party is $400 and about 160 photos on the hourly rate, or $853.75 and unlimited on Celebrations. Pick based on how much of the night you want back.
- **It is a wedding.** Weddings are not hourly work, they are planned productions with scouting, timelines, and backups. I wrote a full honest breakdown of [what wedding photography costs in Toronto](/blog/intimate-toronto-wedding-photography-cost/) if that is the budget you are working on.
- **You need video too.** That is a different conversation, and I am happy to have it, but not at $100 per hour.
