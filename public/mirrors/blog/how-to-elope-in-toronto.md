## Eloping in Toronto at a glance

**Marriage licence:** $180 from the City of Toronto, no HST. Apply within 90 days of the wedding.

**Officiant:** Included at the downtown City Hall chamber and at Old City Hall. Everywhere else, you arrange your own.

**Witnesses:** Two, aged 18 or older.

**Where:** A City wedding chamber, Old City Hall, a City park with a booking, a backyard, or a restaurant.

**Legal minimum cost:** About $547 at the downtown City Hall chamber: the $180 licence plus $325 plus HST, officiant included.

**Photography:** My Civil Ceremony package is $501.25 for up to 2 hours. The Elopement is $901.25 for up to 4 hours.

## Step 2: Get your marriage licence

The licence comes from the City of Toronto and costs $180. Fill in the online application, then book an in person appointment at City Hall or the North York Civic Centre to have it certified and pay by debit or credit card.

Each of you brings two pieces of original, unexpired government ID showing your legal name and date of birth. Health cards and SIN cards do not count. If either of you was married before, bring official proof of divorce. You apply within 90 days of the wedding, so do not get it too early. The details are on the City's [marriage licence page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/venues-facilities-bookings/getting-married/applying-for-a-marriage-licence/).

## Step 3: Arrange the officiant

At the downtown City Hall chamber, the officiant comes with the booking. At Old City Hall, City staff perform the ceremony. Everywhere else, the civic centre chambers, a park, a backyard or a restaurant, you arrange an officiant who is authorized to perform marriages in Ontario and pay them directly.

This is the real cost difference between the options. The civic centre chambers look cheaper at $136.85, but the room comes without anyone to marry you.

## Step 4: Bring two witnesses

Ontario needs two witnesses aged 18 or older to sign the register. A sibling and a best friend is the classic pair. The downtown chamber does not provide witnesses, so line yours up early.

## What to do after the ceremony

This is the part that makes an elopement feel like a wedding.

**A portrait walk.** From City Hall, Nathan Phillips Square, Old City Hall and Osgoode Hall are all within a ten minute walk, and none of them need a permit for a small session. Sonia and Achyut, and Jashan and Dhillon, both did this exact loop after their City Hall ceremonies.

**A second location.** Harsh and Payal split their day between the Toronto Islands and downtown, where a streetcar slid through the background of their portraits.

**A long lunch or dinner.** Some of my favourite elopement frames are in a restaurant booth after the vows, when everyone finally relaxes.

Before you plan a location, check the permit rule in my [Toronto photo permits table](/toronto-photo-permits/). Most are free for a couple and a camera. A few, like Casa Loma, are not.

## Is eloping right for you?

Eloping suits couples who want to be married, not to host. If the legal ceremony and two or three people you love are the whole point, Toronto makes it easy and affordable. Plenty of couples elope now and throw a bigger party later. Eloping does not close that door.
