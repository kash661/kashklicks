## What colours to wear for engagement photos at a glance

**Start with the place:** Look at the colours behind you first, then pick clothes that stand apart from them or sit inside them.

**Coordinate, do not match:** Stay in one colour family as a couple, with one of you lighter and one darker.

**Use an anchor colour:** Repeat one colour in every look. For Harnoor and Harsh it was black.

**By season:** Fall suits cream, camel, rust, olive, burgundy, navy and black. Winter suits one strong colour against snow. Spring and summer suit lighter, softer tones.

**What to avoid:** Neon, very fine stripes, big logos, and clothes the same colour as your background.

**How many looks:** One per location, matched to it. This day had three places, so three looks.

## Start with the backdrop, not the closet

I ask couples to look at the place before the closet, because the place is in almost every frame. Then there are two ways to choose.

**Stand apart from it.** Pick clothes that contrast with the background. That can be dark against light, like black at pale RC Harris. It can be warm against cool, like red against a blue night. This suits places with big plain surfaces: stone buildings, open sky, water, the city at night.

**Sit inside it.** Pick clothes from the same colour family as the background, like cream and sand among fall leaves. This suits places already full of colour, such as a park in October or a garden in bloom. The photos feel calm and warm, and the setting does the work.

Each has a trade off. Standing apart gives you strong shapes, but it can feel stark in a busy place. Sitting inside feels soft and unified, but go too close to the background and you blend in. When you pick a place from my [Toronto location guide](/location-guide/), look at its colours before you look at your wardrobe.

![Harnoor in a long black dress stands against the yellow buff brick wall at RC Harris, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harnoor-harsh/13.jpg)

## One colour family, two shades

Here is how to coordinate outfits as a couple. Stay in the same colour family, then change the shade and the fabric.

At night, Harnoor's red satin sat beside Harsh's burgundy knit. Both are red. Hers is brighter and has a shine, his is deeper and soft. In High Park, her cream knit sat beside his sand overshirt. Both are warm neutrals, in different shades and different surfaces. That is the pre-wedding shoot dress colour combination I suggest most often: one family, one of you lighter, one of you darker.

![Harnoor in a cream knit hugs Harsh, in sand and black, from behind on a High Park bench, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harnoor-harsh/25.jpg)

If you plan more than one look, add an anchor. An anchor is one colour that shows up in every outfit, a bit like the chorus that comes back in a song. For Harnoor and Harsh it was black: his shirt at RC Harris, his t shirt and trousers and her bag in High Park, and his trousers at night. When you scroll the gallery, the black carries you from one place to the next. That one shared colour is what turns three very different outfits into a single engagement photo colour palette.

## Best colours by season in Toronto

**Fall.** The best colours for fall engagement photos are cream, camel, rust, olive, burgundy, navy and black. The leaves turn warm, so warm neutrals and deep tones sit with them instead of competing. If you plan to stand under orange trees, skip bright orange.

**Winter.** Snow and grey stone are pale and cool, so one strong colour carries the frame, like teal, red or emerald. Pale outfits can disappear into snow. My [winter engagement photos guide](/blog/winter-engagement-photos-toronto/) shows a teal lehenga against snow at RC Harris.

**Spring and summer.** The greens are fresh and the light is brighter, so lighter tones and softer colour work well: soft blues, blush, sage and light neutrals. Save the deep colours for evening or the city.

## Colours that fight the camera

This is my short list of what colours not to wear for engagement photos, and the plain reason for each.

**Neon.** Very bright fabric bounces its colour onto your skin. A neon green top can tint a face green, and that is hard to correct later.

**Very fine stripes and tiny checks.** Small, tight patterns can shimmer or ripple when the photo is shown on a screen.

**Big logos and slogans.** Words pull the eye. People read them before they look at your faces.

**Your background's colour, head to toe.** All green in a green park, or all black on a dark night with no light on you, and you disappear into the place.

**Bright white beside very dark clothes in hard sun.** In harsh midday light it is hard to keep detail in both at once. Cloud makes it easy. Harsh's light trousers and black shirt were simple at RC Harris because the sky was fully overcast.

## Texture is colour's partner

Fabric changes how a colour looks.

**Knit** soaks up light and never shines. In the soft grey light of High Park, Harnoor's cream knit looked warm and soft, and at night Harsh's burgundy sweater stayed a deep, even colour.

**Satin** reflects. At night, Harnoor's red dress picked up the street lamps and the glow of the city, so it shows its shape even in the dark.

**Matte fabric**, like his sand overshirt and his black button shirt, sits between the two.

Putting a shiny fabric next to a soft one is the easiest way to coordinate without matching. Red satin beside burgundy knit is the clearest example in the gallery.

## How many looks, and when to change

My rule is one look per location, matched to that location. This day had three places and three looks. We were at RC Harris from 9:29 AM to 12:12 PM, at High Park from 2:03 PM to 3:52 PM, and at Biidaasige Park from 7:10 PM to 8:22 PM. The gaps between places, about two hours and then about three, left time to change without rushing.
