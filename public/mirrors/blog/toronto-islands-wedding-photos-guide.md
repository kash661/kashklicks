Harsh and Payal got married in Toronto and spent the day getting steadily further from it.

They started downtown, on a street corner, kissing while a streetcar smeared past behind them. Then a restaurant booth. Then the ferry, with the CN Tower sitting over their shoulders. By the end of the day they were barefoot on a beach with candles and a cake and nobody else around.

That is the argument for the Toronto Islands. You are fifteen minutes from the middle of the country's biggest city and it feels like a different province.

![A couple running through a Toronto Islands field with the CN Tower on the horizon, motion blurred, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harsh-payal/08.jpg)

If you are planning a wedding ceremony or a photo session on the islands, this is a photographer's guide. The permit you actually need, the ferry timing that ruins days, and where the good light is.

## Start with the permit, because this one is real

The Toronto Islands are City of Toronto parkland, and the City treats formal wedding and engagement photography as a permitted use. This catches people out, because the parks are free to walk into and the internet is full of couples insisting nobody ever asked them for paperwork.

Here is what the City actually lists:

**Wedding photography at Toronto Island Park:** $173.36 including taxes for two hours, maximum 30 people.

**A wedding ceremony at Toronto Island Park:** $260.07 per hour including taxes, maximum 100 people, with availability handled by phone rather than a straight online booking.

**All Outdoor Park Locations**, if you want to roam rather than commit to one site: $485.47 for two hours.

Outdoor ceremony sites are weather and availability dependent and typically run **May 15 to September 30**.

Booking goes through Parks, Forestry and Recreation, Client Services, Park Bookings. Confirm the current numbers before you budget, because City fees move.

Worth knowing how different this is from the alternative: [Niagara Parks charges nothing at all for outdoor wedding photography](/blog/niagara-parks-botanical-gardens-wedding-photographer-guide/) and requires no permit. Toronto charges and permits. Two hours on the islands costs less than the ferry tickets for a big guest list, so this is not a reason to avoid the location, but it is a line item you should know about rather than discover.

## The ferry is the thing that goes wrong

Everything else about an island wedding is easy. The ferry is where days get broken.

Boats leave from the **Jack Layton Ferry Terminal at 9 Queens Quay West**, at Bay Street. Fares are round trip and include the return, so nobody needs a second ticket to get home: **$9.57 adult, $6.15 older adult or youth, $4.51 junior**, infants free.

In summer the ferries serve **Centre Island, Hanlan's Point and Ward's Island**. In winter, Ward's Island only.

The part that matters: the City flags weekends and holidays as busiest, with peak outbound traffic **10 a.m. to 3 p.m.**, peak return **5 p.m. to 9 p.m.**, and waits that can run **30 to 60 minutes**. That is a normal Saturday, not a disaster scenario.

So:

- Cross early morning or mid afternoon, never at noon on a summer Saturday.
- Add an hour of margin you do not think you need.
- Remember that your guests will not add that margin, so tell them a ferry time that is one boat earlier than the one you need them on.
- There is no general vehicle access. Whatever comes over, comes over by hand.

A dress travels fine. Plan for who is carrying it, and for the fact that the walk from the dock to your spot can be ten or fifteen minutes on grass and sand.

## Where to shoot

The islands are not one location. They are six or seven, and the walk between them is the session.

### The ferry itself

Do not treat the crossing as transit. It is the best establishing shot you will get all day: the couple seated, the skyline receding, the CN Tower framed between them.

![A couple seated on the ferry to the Toronto Islands with the Toronto skyline behind them, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harsh-payal/06.jpg)

Sit on the stern for the skyline. The light is hard in the middle of the day out on the water, so this is one place a bright, high contrast frame actually works.

### The meadows and the farm side

Open grass, wildflowers, daisies, and the odd red farm wagon near Centreville. This is where you get scale and space, and where the CN Tower sits small on the horizon instead of dominating.

It is also the best ground for movement. Long lens, slow shutter, let them run.

### The pier and the boardwalk

Water, sailboats, the skyline across the harbour, and enough width for a group. If you have a wedding party, this is where you photograph them.

![A wedding group in coordinated blush outfits on the Toronto Islands pier, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harsh-payal/18.jpg)

### The tree tunnels

The avenues between the islands are long, straight, and shaded, which makes them the answer to a midday ceremony. Walk toward the camera, let the canopy do the work.

### Ward's Island and Hanlan's Point beaches

The best light on the islands, full stop. Dune grass, open sand, and a west facing horizon that goes gold for the last two hours of the day.

![A couple kissing under a fringed umbrella on the beach at the Toronto Islands, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harsh-payal/27.jpg)

If you only get one hour on the islands, spend it here at sunset.

## Practical notes

**Chairs, arches, amplified music, alcohol and confetti** all depend on your specific permitted site and are not blanket-allowed. Ask the permit office about each one you are planning, in writing, rather than assuming.

**Crowds** are real from late morning to mid afternoon on summer weekends. Early morning on the islands is close to empty and the light is beautiful. A weekday is better again.

**There is very little shelter.** Have a plan for rain that is not "we will figure it out," because you cannot quickly relocate from an island.

**Bring water and comfortable shoes.** Harsh and Payal both wore pink sneakers the entire day, which is the correct answer.

![Detail of a couple's matching pink sneakers side by side on the rocks, Toronto Islands, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/harsh-payal/16.jpg)

## Who the islands suit

They suit couples having a small ceremony, or no ceremony at all. They suit people who want their photographs to look like a day out rather than a production. They suit anyone who wants the Toronto skyline in the pictures without standing in traffic to get it.

They do not suit a large formal wedding with a tight schedule, elderly guests who cannot manage a walk, or anyone who needs a guaranteed indoor backup.

Harsh and Payal's day finished with a picnic blanket, candles, a cake and the water going flat behind them, and it is the best possible advertisement for doing this the simple way.
