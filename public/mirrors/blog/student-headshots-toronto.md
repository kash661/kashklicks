A recent corporate session took me out to Albion Public Library. No studio and no lights, just the building and the sidewalk out front.

We started against the glass. The entrance side of Albion is close to floor to ceiling glazing, and a wall of glass that size bounces daylight back at you like a softbox turned sideways. Then we moved a few steps to the wood benches on the plaza for the seated frames, and a few more to the sidewalk for full length. The timber beams that run out over the covered entrance gave us a ceiling, which is the thing most outdoor headshots are missing.

I keep going back to buildings like this instead of renting a studio. A studio gives you one look. A building like Albion gives you three or four in the space of ten steps, in daylight that already looks like the world your photo will live in.

## Why Albion Public Library works for headshots

The branch sits at 1515 Albion Road in north Etobicoke, near Albion Road and Kipling Avenue, in the part of the city most people still call Rexdale. The current building opened in 2017, designed by Perkins and Will with Andrew Frontini as lead architect, and it replaced the 1973 branch that stood on the same site. It won a Toronto Urban Design Award of Excellence in 2019, and standing in front of it you can see why.

What that means for a headshot is light and variety. Roughly forty percent of the envelope is glass, and the glazing opens right up at the corners and at the recessed entrance, so you get a soft even wash on the face without a single light stand. The facade runs in vertical stripes of glazed terracotta tile in grey, chartreuse, orange and fuchsia, which gives you a background with real colour in it when you want one and a quiet neutral one when you step around the corner. The exposed timber beams start over the covered entrance and carry on inside, so there is warmth overhead instead of open sky.

Then the benches on the plaza, a few steps away. Sitting changes people. Shoulders drop, and the smile stops being something they are holding.
