He cupped her face with one hand, and she leaned in until their noses almost touched. Behind them the sun was coming straight through the trees, low and gold, and the haze it made ran across the whole frame. It was 6:00 pm on a Thursday at the end of September, and we were eight minutes into the last hour of light at Guild Park & Gardens.

![Sunburst through the trees as the groom cups the bride's face for an almost kiss at Guild Park & Gardens, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/04.jpg)

This was a styled wedding shoot, not a real wedding day. I planned it to answer one question couples ask me about this park more than any other: what happens here at sunset? So I took a ball gown, a cathedral veil, a black tuxedo, one white calla lily and an old film camera into the ruins, started 80 minutes before the sun went down, and photographed until it was gone.

This post is what I learned. When to start, what the light does minute by minute, where to stand, and the estate gardens around Toronto that give you the same dreamy, old world feeling, so you can compare.

## Guild Park at sunset, at a glance

**Location:** 201 Guildwood Parkway, Scarborough, on top of the Scarborough Bluffs in Toronto's east end.

**What it is:** A public City of Toronto park, free to walk into and open year round. More than 70 columns, arches and carved facades rescued from downtown buildings as they were torn down after the Second World War, set on open lawn among gardens and tall pines.

**This shoot:** Thursday, September 24, 2026. First frame 5:52 pm. Sunset about 7:10 pm. Last frame 7:15 pm.

**Where the sun goes:** West, inland, behind the tree line. Never over the lake, which sits to the south.

**When to start:** About 80 minutes before sunset.

**Permits:** Wedding photography in the park is a City booking, listed at $485.47 for two hours including tax. Engagement sessions need no permit. My [guide to booking a Toronto park photo permit](/blog/toronto-park-photo-permit-how-to-book/) walks through it.

**Parking:** Free on site. About 25 to 35 minutes from downtown by car.

## What the light did, minute by minute

**5:52 pm, the curved stone wall.** We started with the couple holding hands in front of the long carved wall near the entrance. The sun was still above the trees, low enough to rake across the stone and pick out every carved scroll.

**6:00 pm, the veil.** This is the window I came for. With the sun coming through the trees from behind her, I had the bride lift her veil and hold it up to the light. Tulle glows when it is backlit. It turns gold, and it softens everything behind it.

![Bride in profile lifting her veil to the low evening sun at Guild Park, the tulle glowing gold, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/03.jpg)

I spent those minutes on the blusher veil too. The groom lifting it, the two of them nose to nose underneath it, the beams coming through the branches above. If you only get one thing from the first half hour, get this. It is the most dreamy light the park has.

**6:10 pm, side light on the lawn.** As the sun dropped into the trees it stopped coming at the lens and started coming from the side. That is when I took the forehead kiss: her eyes closed, his hand along her jaw, warm light on one side of both faces.

**6:25 pm, the colonnade.** By now the sun was behind the tree line, and the whole park went soft. This is the part people underestimate. Pale stone in direct sun is hard to photograph, it throws harsh shadows and burns out. In this light it glows evenly, and the fluted columns read in full detail from top to bottom.

![Bride and groom at arm's length about to twirl in front of the Guild Park colonnade in soft evening light, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/11.jpg)

**6:30 to 6:40 pm, the carved archway and the steps.** The archway with the bearded face carved at its keystone is the frame I come back to most at Guild Park, and in even light you can work it from every side. A twirl framed inside the arch. A lift beneath it with the tulle fanning out. The bride sitting on the stone steps with the veil spread down them.

**6:45 pm, the twirls.** Out on the open lawn, with the hedges behind, I let her spin. The ball gown and the cathedral veil are made for this.

![Bride mid twirl laughing with her eyes closed, veil lifted and gown flaring on the lawn at Guild Park, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/23.jpg)

**6:48 to 6:50 pm, the groom.** A few frames of him alone at the base of a column, fixing a cufflink, then throwing his jacket off. Ten minutes in the right light is plenty.

**6:54 to 7:03 pm, the details.** Rings balanced on her collarbone next to the necklace. Rings stacked on the stem of the calla lily, with the columns blurred behind. A white cape over the gown for the white park bench. Details love this light because nothing is shining back at the lens.

![Engagement ring and wedding band stacked on a white calla lily stem with the columns soft behind, at Guild Park, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/29.jpg)

**7:08 to 7:15 pm, the last frames.** A chase across the lawn under the colonnade. Then the bride lying back in the grass with her head in his lap and the gown pooled around them. The sun set at about ten past seven. The very last frame was at 7:15, and by then the columns behind them were going grey.

![Bride's gown pooled across the lawn as the groom leans over her, columns and carved arch behind, at Guild Park at sunset, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/32.jpg)

## Why it looks dreamy, and how I do it on purpose

A lot of these photos are soft on purpose. That is not the camera missing focus.

**I drag the shutter.** For some frames I slow the shutter down and move with the couple, so the gown and the veil smear across the frame while a face or a hand stays readable. On the steps I got the bride sitting still and the groom standing in the arch as two ghosts. It looks the way the evening felt, which is why I shoot it.

![Slow shutter ghosting of the bride seated on the stone steps and the groom standing in the carved archway at Guild Park, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/guild-park-sunset-styled-wedding/14.jpg)

**I focus on the stone.** In a few frames the sharp thing is the carving, and the couple sits soft in front of it. At Guild Park the ruins are half the story, so sometimes I give them the focus.

**I keep the grain.** The edit is warm and grainy, closer to film than to a clean digital file. Whimsical, a little old, a little imperfect. It suits a place built from the stones of old Toronto.

If you want every photo crisp and still, tell me. I will shoot it that way. But if you came to this post for the dreamy feeling, this is how it is made.

## Planning your own sunset session at Guild Park

**Start time.** Take the sunset time for your date and subtract 80 minutes. Here is roughly when the sun sets at Guild Park through the season:

- May 1: about 8:15 pm
- June 15: about 9:00 pm, the latest of the year
- July 15: about 8:55 pm
- August 15: about 8:20 pm
- September 1: about 7:55 pm
- September 15: about 7:30 pm
- October 1: about 7:00 pm
- October 15: about 6:35 pm

Check the exact time a week before. The light will not wait for a late arrival.

**Order of spots.** Backlit work first, while the sun is still in the trees: the veil, anything with flare. Then the colonnade, the archway and the steps once the light goes soft. Save the open lawn and the gown on the grass for the end, when everything is even and low.

**What to wear.** Anything with movement. A full skirt, a long veil, a cape or a scarf. Fabric is what catches the backlight and what blurs beautifully when I drag the shutter. In late September a layer for after sunset is worth bringing.

**Props.** Keep them small and old. One flower. A film camera. A bench is already there.

**Permits.** For wedding photography in the park, book the City permit (above). For an engagement session, you do not need one. If your reception is at the Guild Inn Estate inside the park, ask the estate what their booking covers before paying the City for anything.

**After the sun goes.** The blue light after sunset lasts a little while, but in the ruins it gets dark fast under the trees. Plan to finish within five to ten minutes of sunset.

## Estate gardens near Toronto with the same feeling

Guild Park is one of a handful of places around Toronto that look like an old European estate. If you love these photos, these are the other estates and gardens I have photographed that sit in the same family. One big difference to watch: not all of them let you stay for sunset.

**[Parkwood Estate](/blog/parkwood-estate-engagement-photos-oshawa/), Oshawa.** R.S. McLaughlin's 1917 estate, 11 acres of formal gardens, a colonnade, a long turquoise pool between clipped cedars. Of everything on this list it is the closest to Guild Park in mood. But it is a daylight location: in summer and in October the gardens close at 5 pm, and you need a photo pass ($300 plus HST for two hours outdoors). For a Guild Park look in full bloom, Parkwood is the place. For a Guild Park look at sunset, Guild Park is.

![Parneet laughing as Maninder leans in close between stone columns at Parkwood Estate in Oshawa, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/maninder-parneet/02.jpg)

**[Adamson Estate](/blog/adamson-estate-wedding-photography-guide/), Mississauga.** A 1919 stone manor on the lake at 850 Enola Avenue, with a terrace, brick courtyards and archways. The lakefront terrace gets bounce light off the water in the last two hours before sunset, and in early summer the amber light between about 7:45 and 8:15 is the best of the day. The City of Mississauga asks for a commercial photography permit for professional shoots, so call 905-615-4100 first.

![Golden hour light warming Ayushi and Parth as they look toward the setting sun at Adamson Estate in Mississauga, by AD Photography](../../assets/images/portfolio/ayushi-parth-adamson/18.jpg)

**[Dundurn Castle](/blog/dundurn-castle-wedding-photographer-guide/), Hamilton.** An 1830s Italianate villa with a columned portico and a tree lined drive above Hamilton Harbour. In golden hour the drive backlights and the stone goes warm. It is a City of Hamilton historic site, so confirm booking and hours with Dundurn directly.

**[The Glenerin Inn](/blog/glenerin-inn-wedding-photographer-guide/), Mississauga.** A stone manor in a wooded ravine, with a ceremony deck under old maples. It is a full wedding venue, so photos on the grounds come with your booking. The light through the maples in late afternoon is soft and moving, the same quality the Guild Park pines give you early in the evening.

**[Niagara Parks Botanical Gardens](/blog/niagara-parks-botanical-gardens-wedding-photographer-guide/), Niagara Falls.** Less ruin, more garden. Willows, tree lined paths and a glass conservatory. Niagara Parks needs no permit for ordinary outdoor photos. It is about 125 to 130 km from downtown Toronto, so plan the drive around sunset, not the other way round.

**[Château des Charmes](/blog/chateau-des-charmes-proposal-photography/), Niagara on the Lake.** A limestone château with a front fountain and rose beds among the vines. I photographed a proposal there. It has the same "did we fly somewhere?" feeling.

**And at Guild Park itself, the Guild Inn Estate.** The restored 1914 house inside the park is where seated ceremonies and receptions happen. A wedding there gives you dinner at the estate and the ruins at sunset a few minutes' walk away. That is exactly how [Josie and Nelson's Guild Park wedding](/blog/guild-park-wedding-josie-nelson/) worked.

A few other estates come up all the time when couples plan garden weddings around Toronto: [Graydon Hall Manor](/location-guide/graydon-hall/) in North York, [Casa Loma](/location-guide/casa-loma/), and [Paletta Mansion](/location-guide/paletta/) in Burlington. I have not photographed a wedding at those yet, so I will not pretend to know their light the way I know the places above.
